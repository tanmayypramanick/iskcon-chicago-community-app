Who runs the app

The app is run by ISKCON Chicago (the International Society for Krishna Consciousness of Chicago), a religious congregation in Illinois, USA. The temple is the controller of the information described here. It is not a commercial product, there is no advertising in it, and no information in it is sold or rented to anybody.

Every screen beyond the sign-in page requires an account. There is no public devotee profile and no guest directory.

What we collect

Almost everything below is something you type in yourself. Nothing is gathered quietly in the background.

Your account

  • Your name, email address and a contact phone number, which is required
  • Your password, which is stored by our hosting provider as a hash and is never visible to us
  • Your profile photo, if you add one
  • Your access level — Devotee, Volunteer, Community Head, Tech Admin or President — and the date you joined
  • If you sign in with Google, your name, email address and profile picture as Google provides them

Your personal details

  • Date of birth, place of birth, gender and occupation
  • Home address
  • Marital status, your spouse's name, and details of your children — each child's name, age, gender, whether they practise and since when
  • Emergency contact name and phone number
  • Dietary notes and health notes, if you choose to write them
  • Languages you speak, your preferred language, skills you offer, whether you can give a lift or host a program, and how you found the temple

Your spiritual details

  • Whether you are initiated, your first and second initiation dates, and your diksha gurus
  • Your spiritual mentor
  • Your daily rounds of chanting
  • How long you have been coming to the temple

Your seva

  • The seva you join, are invited to, register yourself, or step back from
  • Whether a coordinator recorded you as served, absent or excused
  • Start and finish times, including seva started by scanning a QR code, and the running timer
  • Who verified a seva you registered yourself, and any note they left
  • Coverage you asked for or agreed to cover
  • What you wrote when you registered interest in weekly seva — your skills and availability
  • Your seva hours, seva count, giving total and any awards, used for Seva Yatra and the leaderboard

Being at the temple

  • The At the temple switch, which is yours to turn on and off
  • The date, and the times you checked in and checked out

No coordinate, address or map position is ever recorded. Turning the switch off takes you off today's list at once.

Messages and sangas

  • Direct messages you send and receive — the text, any photo, and the times they were sent, delivered and read
  • Sanga messages and photos, and which sangas you belong to
  • The original text and photo of a message you delete — see how long it is kept

Donations

  • Your name and email as given to Zeffy, the amount, the currency, whether it was one-time or recurring, and which campaign it was for
  • Zeffy's payment reference, and the payment record Zeffy returns to us, which is kept for reconciliation and is readable only by the temple's server
  • Sponsorship bookings and whether they were fulfilled

Payment itself happens on Zeffy's own page in your phone's browser. The app never sees or stores a card number.

Notifications

  • A push token issued by Expo for the app on your phone
  • Whether the phone is an iPhone or an Android phone, and the device name it reports — for example “Radha's iPhone”
  • When that token was last seen, so tokens for phones you no longer use can be retired
  • The notifications sent to you and whether you have read them

Photos and files

  • Your profile photo
  • Photos you attach to a message or a sanga chat
  • Daily darshan photos, and the name recorded for who dressed the Deities
  • Newsletter files, and anything you submit for the newsletter

All of these are held in private storage. Opening one requires a signed-in account.

What you write elsewhere

  • Announcements, comments and birthday wishes
  • Feedback you send the temple
  • Devotee care posts and replies, which are confidential to the devotees authorised to answer them
  • Requests for a higher access level, and the decision on them

What we do not collect

This part matters as much as the list above, so it is stated on its own.

  • Your location. The app does not read, send or store where your phone is. There is no background location, no geofence and no location permission. Temple check-in is a switch you turn on yourself.
  • Card and bank details. Donations are completed on Zeffy's own page in your phone's browser. Nothing about your card reaches the app or the temple's database.
  • Analytics and tracking. There is no analytics product in the app, no advertising identifier, no third-party tracker and no cross-app tracking of any kind.
  • Crash reports. When something goes wrong the app keeps the last few error details in memory so it can show you a sensible message. They are never sent anywhere and they are gone when the app closes.
  • Your contacts, calendar, microphone or camera roll at large. The app asks for a photo only at the moment you choose to attach one, and receives only the photo you picked.

Why we hold it

A temple has always kept a record of its congregation, and this app is that record in a form a devotee can read and correct. Each thing is held for a reason that can be named:

  • To let you in. Your email, password and access level are what sign you in and decide what you may do.
  • To reach you. Your phone number and email let a coordinator contact you about seva you agreed to; your push token is how a reminder arrives.
  • To arrange seva. Availability, skills, attendance and hours are what a coordinator works from when filling a rota.
  • To offer seva that suits you. Initiation, mentor and rounds let weekly seva be offered that fits where you are in your practice. None of it is a requirement for seva.
  • To look after you. Emergency contact, dietary and health notes are held so the temple can respond sensibly if something happens during a program or a seva.
  • To keep the community's records. Donations, sponsorships, messages and care posts are kept as temple records for accountability and continuity.
  • To meet obligations. Donation records are kept for the temple's accounts and for tax and charity reporting.

Under laws that ask for a legal basis, we rely on your consent for the details you volunteer, on the legitimate interests of a religious congregation in running its community, and on legal obligation for donation records. Health notes and spiritual details are given voluntarily and are held on the basis of your consent; you may leave both blank.

Who can see it

The honest summary is that the temple's leadership can see a devotee's record. That is how a temple works, and it is stated here plainly rather than buried. What the wider community sees is much narrower.

Who What they can see
Any signed-in devotee Your name, photo, access level and joined date, and — on your profile page — your email address, gender, date of birth, age and occupation. They also see you on today's temple list while your At the temple switch is on, your name beside seva you share, and your messages in any sanga you both belong to.
A devotee you message What you send them, and any photo attached to it.
Whoever posted a seva Who joined it, and whether each person served, was absent or was excused. If you are marked as not attending, the app tells you, so you can say if it is wrong.
Community Heads The full seva schedule and devotees' completed seva, because arranging seva depends on it.
The President and Tech Admin Everything in your record. That includes your phone number, home address, birth place, initiation and mentor; every direct message and sanga message, including the original text and photo of messages that were deleted; every devotee's seva and attendance; and every donation. This is deliberate — it is what lets the temple answer a safeguarding question or an accounts question — and it is not something you can switch off in the app.
Newsletter editors Submissions made for the newsletter, and who sent them.
Devotees authorised for care Confidential devotee care posts and replies.
Nobody else Your health notes, dietary notes, emergency contact, marital status, spouse, children, chanting rounds and skills never appear in the Directory or on any profile page.

The app has no per-field privacy switches yet. The two things genuinely in your hands are the At the temple switch and whether you appear on the Seva Yatra leaderboard. Rather than show toggles that change nothing, the app's Privacy and visibility screen explains what is held — and this policy does the same.

Who else it passes through

We do not sell, rent or trade anything about you. These are the services the app needs to work, and what each one handles.

Service What it handles
Supabase Our hosting processor. It runs the sign-in, the database, the file storage and the server functions, so everything described in this policy is stored on Supabase's infrastructure on the temple's behalf. Data is held in the United States.
Expo Issues the push token for your phone and delivers our notifications, which means the title and body of a notification pass through Expo's push service.
Apple and Google Apple's push service on iPhone and Firebase Cloud Messaging on Android carry the notification the last step to your phone.
Zeffy Takes the donation on its own page and tells the temple that it happened. Zeffy's privacy policy governs what you enter there.
Google Sign-In Only if you choose it. Google tells us your name, email address and profile picture; we tell Google nothing about you.

We will also disclose information if the law requires it, or where it is genuinely necessary to protect someone's safety.

Children

The app is meant for adults. We do not knowingly create accounts for children under 13, and a child should not sign up.

A parent or guardian may record their children in their own profile — a child's name, age, gender, whether they practise and since when — as part of the family's congregation record. That is the parent's choice and can be left blank or cleared at any time in Profile details. It is never shown in the Directory or to other devotees.

If you believe a child has created an account, or that a child's details are in the app and should not be, write to us at the address below and we will remove them.

How it is kept safe

  • Everything travels over an encrypted connection, and is encrypted where it is stored.
  • Passwords are hashed by our hosting provider. Nobody at the temple can read your password.
  • The database enforces who may read each row, on the server, rather than trusting the app to hide things. A devotee's request for someone else's private details is refused by the database itself.
  • Photos, message images and newsletter files sit in private storage; a link to one does not work without a signed-in account.
  • Access levels are granted by the President or a Tech Admin, and every request and decision is recorded.
  • No card details are ever held, because no card details ever arrive.

No system is perfect, and we do not claim this one is. If you think an account has been used by someone else, tell us and we will act.

How long it is kept

Your record is kept for as long as you are part of the community, and the temple's records of seva, donations and community life are kept indefinitely, as a temple's records always have been. There is no automatic clear-out.

Two things deserve to be said outright, because they are not what people usually assume:

  • Deleting a message does not destroy it. Deleting removes it from the conversation both devotees see, but the original text and photo are retained as a temple record and can be read by the President or a Tech Admin.
  • Removing a conversation only clears your own view. It empties that thread from your Messages list and nothing more. A new message brings the conversation back.

Feedback you send and newsletter submissions you make are the exception: deleting one of those really does delete it. Donation records are kept for at least seven years for the temple's accounts.

Your rights, leaving and being forgotten

You can see and change most of what the app holds about you at any time in Profile details. What you save takes effect straight away, everywhere the app shows it.

You may ask us to:

  • tell you what we hold about you, and give you a copy;
  • correct anything that is wrong;
  • erase your account and the personal details in it — see below;
  • stop sending you notifications — you can also turn banners off in your phone's settings, and the app's Notification settings screen lists every kind we send;
  • object to how we use something, or ask us to restrict it.

Leaving, and being forgotten, are two different things

Leaving is stepping away from the app. Nothing is erased. Notifications stop, and the temple keeps your record as it stands, the way a congregation roll has always been kept. Sign in again and everything is where you left it.

Being forgotten is asking the temple to stop holding who you are. Every personal detail goes — your name, email, phone, address, date of birth, photo, health and dietary notes, emergency contact and family. Your sign-in stops working and cannot be restored.

Both are in the app, under Profile → Leaving, or deleting your account. Being forgotten asks you to type DELETE first, because it cannot be undone and there is nothing to restore from afterwards. If you would rather ask a person, write to [TEMPLE PRIVACY EMAIL — to be filled in] from the address on your account and we will confirm it is you, then carry it out.

Two things outlive being forgotten, and neither of them says who you were: the record of seva that was performed, because a month's hours must still add up a year later; and the record of donations received, which a charity in the United States must keep for its accounts and for tax. Your name is removed from both.

We answer within 30 days. There is no charge, and asking will never affect how you are treated at the temple. Depending on where you live you may also have the right to complain to a data protection authority; in Illinois you may contact the Office of the Illinois Attorney General.
